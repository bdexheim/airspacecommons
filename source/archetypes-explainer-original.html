Chicago builds one airspace commons that the drone industry pays for, every agency reads from, and the people under the flights can see, question and refuse.

What has to change

Six conditions. None exists today.

The commons holds as far as the city's ground lever reaches, and only if the burden budget really caps total flying. Both mandates rest on it. The city can enforce a cap only through the ground it controls: launch sites, hubs and permits.

A burden budget the city can enforce

Tied to siting, launch-site access and permits, counted across every agency's flights, never loosened as the industry grows.

An owner that isn't a tenant

DTI hosts the platform. An independent custodian board, created by ordinance, decides who may use the data.

Purpose rules with teeth

A closed list of purposes, a public vote to add one, a never-allowed list, carrier eligibility, and data minimized at the source.

A named arbiter

OEMC in emergencies. For everyday conflicts: the protected burden share first, then purpose, then first come.

A cost split

The base is funded centrally, add-ons are paid by whoever asks, regulated parties pay for their own impacts.

Paid participation

Stipends for crews and panel members, and a seating rule that puts the highest-burden blocks first.

Archetype 1 without 3 is surveillance with good terms. Archetype 2 without 3 is efficient exposure. Archetype 3 without 1 and 2 has nothing to govern.
